---
title: The primitive contract
description: How every GridLayout part renders, takes your props and exposes its state, so you can style and compose it freely.
---

Every part follows the same rules.

- **`render`, never `asChild`.** `render={<section />}` merges the part's props into your element;
  `render={(props, state) => …}` hands you both.
- **`ref` is a plain prop**, merged with the part's own.
- **Your props are forwarded**, and your handlers run after the part's own. On `Root`, the grid's
  pointer and key handling runs after yours: `preventDefault` vetoes a gesture or replaces a key.
- **`className` and `style` take a value or a function of the state.** Your style sits under the
  part's structural style.
- **Structural inline style only**: a position, a size, a transform, the held item above the others,
  `touch-action: none` on handles and drag sources, and a placeholder and drag preview that never
  take the pointer. Nothing cosmetic.
- **State through `data-*` and ARIA**, present or absent, never `"false"`.
  See [State attributes](/docs/concepts/state-attributes).
- **No text and no names.** Give every item, handle and button its accessible name.
- **You own the recursion.** `GridLayout.Items` is a children function over the layout's items.

## The parts

| part | hook | what it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `GridLayout.Root` | `useGridLayout`, `useGridLayoutView` | the positioned box the items are placed in |
| `GridLayout.Items` | `useItems` | a children function over the items, keyed by id |
| `GridLayout.Item` | `useItem` | an item, placed at its box; a tab stop |
| `GridLayout.DragHandle` | `useDragHandle` | the only place its item drags from, and its tab stop |
| `GridLayout.ResizeHandle` | `useResizeHandle` | a side or corner the item is resized from |
| `GridLayout.Placeholder` | `usePlaceholder` | where the held item would land, during a gesture |
| `GridLayout.DragSource` | `useDragSource` | anything on the page that brings a new item in; a tab stop |
| `GridLayout.DragPreview` | `useDragPreview` | what follows the pointer while it brings an item in |

A part hook returns `{ state, props }`: spread `props` onto any element to make it that part.

Outside the root, parts and hooks reach the grid through a `gridLayoutRef`
(`useGridLayoutRef()`, or `createGridLayoutRef()` outside a component), given to the root too.

## In Vue

`@fragiola/grid-layout-vue` keeps the same contract, written the Vue way
([Vue components](/docs/api/vue)):

- `render` is `as` (a tag or a component) or the `render` scoped slot, which receives the props
  to bind and the state.
- Attributes and listeners fall through onto the part's element, under its structural style; a
  part's own listeners compose with yours.
- `class` and `style` take a value, not a function of the state: style the state through the
  `data-*` attributes (`data-dragging:shadow-lg`, `[data-dragging]` in CSS), or read it from the
  `render` slot.
- `GridLayout.Items` is a scoped slot over the layout's items (`v-slot="{ item }"`): you still own
  what each item renders.
- A composable returns `{ state, props }` as computed refs: bind `props` with `v-bind`.

## In Angular

`@fragiola/grid-layout-angular` keeps the same contract, written the Angular way
([Angular directives](/docs/api/angular)):

- `render` is the element you write: each part is a directive on it (`gridLayoutRoot`,
  `[gridLayoutItem]="item.id"`, `gridLayoutDragHandle`, `[gridLayoutDragSource]="{ w, h }"`),
  which keeps your attributes, classes, styles and listeners; the structural style keys win.
- `className` and `style` as a function of the state are your own bindings, reading the part's
  `state()` signal through `exportAs` (`#part="gridLayoutItem"`,
  `[class.held]="part.state().dragging"`), or a `data-*` selector.
- The recursion, and the parts present only at times, are template directives:
  `<ng-template gridLayoutItems let-item>`, `*gridLayoutCells="let cell"`,
  `*gridLayoutPlaceholder`, `*gridLayoutResizeHandle="'bottom-end'"`, `*gridLayoutDragPreview`.
- Callbacks are outputs (`(layoutChange)`, `(dragStart)`, `(dropped)`, …); `[(layout)]` binds the
  layout both ways, once per committed change.
- Hooks are injection functions that return signals (`injectGridLayout()`, `injectItems()`, …);
  a part's function (`injectItem()`) gives the enclosing directive's `{ state }`.
